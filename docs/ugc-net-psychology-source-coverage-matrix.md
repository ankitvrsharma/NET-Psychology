> Status: first-pass audit matrix, not a claim that the unit or any topic is complete. The ten-unit outline is preserved. Source roles are distinct: syllabus bounds scope; textbooks teach; topic PDFs deepen selected areas; PYQs evidence question demands. Verify exact page/chapter citations before learner-facing publication.

## How to use this matrix

For every micro-topic, track: (1) syllabus alignment, (2) conceptual source and page/chapter, (3) specialist notes and page, (4) authentic PYQ stem/part and exact source page, (5) learner explanation, (6) contrast/confusion pair, (7) retrieval prompt and answer, (8) application item, (9) status: covered / partial / missing / duplicate / conflict. A title or templated paragraph is not coverage. PYQ frequency prioritises work but does not remove low-frequency syllabus concepts.

## Completion standard from an aspirant's perspective

A learner should be able to define and explain the construct; name relevant theorists/models/instruments; describe stages, assumptions, dimensions or mechanisms; distinguish near-neighbours; apply the concept to a scenario; interpret a graph, score, formula or study where relevant; and answer a syllabus-aligned question without relying on recognition alone. If one record contains multiple independent objectives, sequence or split the teaching without breaking stable IDs or saved learner state. If two records teach the same objective, consolidate only after checking the syllabus map and data-migration implications.

## Unit 1 — Emergence of Psychology

**Existing coverage clusters:** Eastern/Indic traditions; development of academic psychology in India; colonialism and disciplinary identity; Greek, medieval and modern thought; schools/movements; Wundt, Freud, James and Dilthey; paradigms and philosophy of science; science/spirituality and self-knowledge.

**Aspirant must connect:** schools to their assumptions, methods, key contributors and views of mind/behaviour; historical periods to major shifts; ontology, epistemology and methodology to the kind of knowledge claimed; Indian traditions to their own concepts rather than treating them as interchangeable labels.

**Likely confusions to teach explicitly:** structuralism vs functionalism; Gestalt vs behaviourism; psychoanalytic vs humanistic/existential; positivism vs post-positivism vs social constructionism; Indian traditions (Yoga, Buddhism, Sufism, Bhagavad Gita) must not be collapsed into one generic “Eastern psychology” answer.

**Source plan:** Baron & Misra and the Self-Teaching Guide for broad historical/conceptual explanations where chapters support them; PowerWithin for exam-oriented topic inventory; use the repository's Indian psychology source coverage and identify any gaps. PYQ evidence should be linked only where a question is actually found; do not infer PYQ frequency from syllabus headings.

**Chunk test:** a learner can compare two schools/paradigms on assumptions, method and key claim, not just recite their names.

## Unit 2 — Research Methodology and Statistics

**Existing coverage clusters:** research questions, variables, operational definitions, hypotheses, sampling, ethics, quantitative/qualitative/mixed methods, research designs, descriptive statistics, normal curve, parametric/non-parametric tests, power/effect size, correlation/regression, factor analysis, ANOVA and related designs.

**Aspirant must connect:** research question → design → operationalisation/measurement → sample → data type → suitable analysis → interpretation/limitations. Teach assumptions and decision rules alongside definitions. Keep statistical inference separate from substantive importance.

**PYQ evidence visible in the uploaded compilation:** sampling (Part 7); standardized-score interpretation (Part 3; measurement/testing crossover); degrees of freedom, levels of data, item analysis, multiple regression, fixed effects/randomized block design, Bartlett's test and variance explained appear as named topic headings across the PYQ set. These headings are search leads, not proof of a complete question or answer key.

**High-value contrasts:** probability vs non-probability sampling; nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio; Type I vs Type II error; statistical significance vs effect size vs power; correlation vs regression; between-subjects vs within-subjects; parametric vs non-parametric tests; item difficulty vs discrimination.

**Source plan:** use the PowerWithin book and relevant methods/statistics chapters in the core textbooks; Paper I research aptitude notes may supplement shared methods but must be labelled Paper I and not used to expand Paper II. Record exact section/page for formulas and assumptions.

**Chunk test:** learner chooses and justifies an analysis/design for a short scenario, interprets output and identifies the relevant assumption or limitation.

## Unit 3 — Psychological Testing

**Existing coverage clusters:** test types/construction, item writing and analysis, standardisation, reliability, validity, norms and standard scores; intelligence, creativity, neuropsychological, aptitude, personality and interest assessment; major instruments; attitude scales and computer-based testing; applications.

**Aspirant must connect:** construct → intended population/use → item/administration → reliability evidence → validity evidence → norms/interpretation → ethical/application limits. Test names should be taught with author/purpose, target group, format, scoring and limitations only when supported by sources.

**PYQ crossovers:** standardized scores and percentile ranks (Part 3); item difficulty/discrimination and semantic differential (parts to verify against source page); EPQ vs NEO-PI and WAIS are explicit retrieval targets in the uploaded PYQ headings.

**High-value contrasts:** reliability vs validity; criterion vs construct vs content validity; norm-referenced vs criterion-referenced; ability vs achievement vs aptitude; projective vs objective inventories; Likert vs semantic differential vs Stapel; score conversion vs percentile rank.

**Source plan:** core textbook testing/assessment chapters and PowerWithin; verify instrument details from source text before publishing. Avoid generic “test is reliable/valid” statements without the type and evidence.

**Chunk test:** learner interprets what a score means and what it cannot establish, and selects a suitable test for a stated purpose.

## Unit 4 — Biological Basis of Behavior

**Existing coverage clusters:** sensory systems, neurons and transmission, neurotransmitters, CNS/PNS, brain structures/lobes, autonomic system, neuroplasticity, physiological methods, muscular/glandular systems, emotion, genetics and motivation.

**Aspirant must connect:** structure → function → pathway/mechanism → behavioural consequence → method/evidence. Use labelled pathways and comparisons where the syllabus calls for anatomy or physiological sequences.

**PYQ crossovers:** cortisol pathway (Part 3), neurotransmitters, neurons/glia, reflexes, long-term potentiation, REM sleep and Parkinson's disease appear as headings in the PYQ collection. Each must be mapped to the correct existing topic and checked against the actual stem.

**High-value contrasts:** CNS vs PNS; sympathetic vs parasympathetic; agonist vs antagonist where supported; endocrine vs neural signalling; REM vs NREM; cortical localisation vs distributed networks; correlation vs causal inference in physiological studies.

**Source plan:** Ciccarelli & White and Baron & Misra for core biological concepts; PowerWithin for syllabus-oriented coverage; cross-check specialised medical terms and pathways before creating explanatory diagrams.

**Chunk test:** learner can trace a pathway or predict a likely consequence from a stated lesion, transmitter change or physiological response without overclaiming causality.

## Unit 5 — Attention, Perception, Learning, Memory and Forgetting

**Existing coverage clusters:** attention; perception and Gestalt organisation; constancy/illusions; depth and movement; signal detection; subliminal/cultural/perceptual styles; conditioning and reinforcement; behaviour modification; observational/verbal/discrimination learning; memory systems and forgetting.

**PYQ evidence:** classical vs operant conditioning (Part 4); signal detection (Part 5); depth perception (Part 1); apparent motion, what/where pathways, proactive/retroactive interference, observational learning, Premack principle, shaping/chaining and REM sleep appear in the uploaded heading set. The perception specialist PDF specifically describes depth perception as three-dimensional distance judgement and references the visual-cliff procedure in infants; preserve that framing and cite the page before expanding.

**High-value contrasts:** sensitivity vs response criterion; hit/miss/false alarm/correct rejection; monocular vs binocular depth cues where sourced; classical vs operant conditioning; negative reinforcement vs punishment; shaping vs chaining; acquisition vs extinction vs spontaneous recovery; encoding vs storage vs retrieval; proactive vs retroactive interference; episodic vs semantic vs procedural memory.

**Source plan:** CHROMEIAS Perception notes plus Ciccarelli & White/Self-Teaching Guide for perception/learning/memory; PowerWithin and actual PYQ pages for exam-demand alignment.

**Chunk test:** learner explains a phenomenon, predicts the effect of a manipulation and rejects the nearest distractor; for signal detection, classify a scenario in the 2×2 outcome table and distinguish sensitivity from criterion.

## Unit 6 — Thinking, Intelligence and Creativity

**Existing coverage clusters:** theories of thought, concepts/reasoning, language, problem solving, metacognition, intelligence theories, creativity theories, intelligence-creativity relation and decision-making.

**Aspirant must connect:** theory/model → core process → predicted behaviour → evidence or task → limitation. Do not reduce theorists to name-matching cards.

**PYQ crossovers:** metacognition, heuristics, linguistic relativity, emotional intelligence and theory of mind are headings in the PYQ compilation; map each to the right syllabus location and note overlap rather than duplicating the whole lesson.

**High-value contrasts:** algorithm vs heuristic; functional fixedness vs mental set; convergent vs divergent thinking; intelligence theories (Spearman, Thurstone, Cattell, Gardner, Sternberg and others) by what each proposes; metacognitive knowledge vs regulation; intelligence vs creativity; bounded rationality vs normative decision models where in scope.

**Source plan:** core textbook cognition/intelligence chapters, Baron & Misra, PowerWithin and PYQ source pages.

**Chunk test:** learner solves or diagnoses a short problem, names the process involved and explains why a competing explanation is weaker.

## Unit 7 — Personality, Motivation, Emotion, Stress and Coping

**Existing coverage clusters:** determinants and personality approaches/theories; trait models and locus of control; motives and motivational theories; curiosity/sensation seeking; achievement/affiliation/power; self-regulation/flow; emotion physiology/theories/regulation; conflict; stress/coping/management; explanatory styles/learned helplessness; Kohlberg.

**Source evidence:** CHROMEIAS Personality Theories notes contain a dedicated theory overview; the stress PDF begins with stress as a psychological and physiological response to a real or perceived threat, involving cognitive, behavioural and biological levels. REVISATHON Part 2 contains a Schachter–Singer component-ordering question. These should be tied to page-specific notes before content is rewritten.

**High-value contrasts:** Freud vs neo-Freudians; trait vs type; Allport vs Cattell vs Eysenck vs Big Five; drive reduction vs incentive/arousal approaches; James–Lange vs Cannon–Bard vs Schachter–Singer vs Lazarus; eustress vs distress; problem-focused vs emotion-focused coping; explanatory style vs learned helplessness.

**Source plan:** CHROMEIAS Personality Theories, stress notes, Baron & Misra, Ciccarelli & White and PowerWithin. Use PYQs to target ordering, attribution and model comparison; do not let a PYQ replace the underlying theory explanation.

**Chunk test:** learner can compare models on sequence/mechanism and apply the model to a scenario, not just memorise the theorist's name.

## Status vocabulary

- **Covered:** learner-facing explanation and retrieval/application checks are source-traceable and validated.
- **Partially covered:** a concept is present but an essential explanation, distinction, source reference or application is missing.
- **Missing:** syllabus-relevant objective has no adequate lesson.
- **Duplicate candidate:** two records may overlap; merge only after mapping dependencies and saved-state risks.
- **Source conflict:** sources differ; document the discrepancy and resolve it before publication.
- **PYQ candidate mapping:** likely related based on title/stem; not a verified mapping until the actual item and lesson are checked.

## Guardrails

Do not change the ten-unit outline. Preserve existing IDs and saved-state references. Keep authentic PYQs verbatim and separate from generated practice questions. Never invent answer keys, years, page references or source agreement.
